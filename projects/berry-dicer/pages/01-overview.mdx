**Two steps, both done to many berries at once: a wire takes the same 5 mm off
every leafy end, then a press pushes the batch through a stainless grid while a
second wire cuts it off underneath every 12 mm.** Everything the fruit touches is
stainless steel and goes in the dishwasher. The printed PLA Pure is frame only.
Modeled, not built.

## Why it works on strawberries

**The awkward shape only matters for the top, and the top is measured from the
leafy end.** Laid on their sides with the leafy end against a fence, big and small
berries all lose 5 mm. Once the tops are off, which way up a berry lies makes no
difference to the grid. Pushing it down 12 mm at a time and cutting underneath
after each step gives cubes, not long sticks. See [how it cuts](./how-it-cuts/).

## Two builds

**A manual pair (a topper and a press) and an automated machine that does both, on
the same stainless parts.**

| | Manual | Automated |
|---|---|---|
| Topping | Swing the wire arm, flip the fence, tip the tray | One button: servos do all three |
| Dicing | Turn the handle a click, push the wire rod, repeat | Long press: lead screws and a belt |
| Your hands | Lay berries in the tray, turn, push | Lay berries in the tray |
| 60 berries a week | ~6.5 min (EST) | ~4.7 min (EST) |
| Parts beyond the stainless | Printed frame, a spring plunger | Three steppers, three servos, a XIAO ESP32S3 |

A knife and board is about 11 minutes for the same 60 (EST). Under about 20
berries a week the knife wins, because the tools take longer to wash. See
[time and force](./physics/).
