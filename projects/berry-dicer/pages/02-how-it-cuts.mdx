---
title: How it cuts
summary: The top is cut from the leafy end; the cubes come from the grid's height, its cells and the step.
---

**Topping: a row of berries lies on its side with every leafy end against a
fence, and a wire comes down 5 mm in front of it.** So the cut follows each berry,
not the tray: a 24 mm berry and a 36 mm berry lose the same 5 mm. Then the flap
holding the tops flips down and drops them in the compost.

**Dicing: the grid is 12 mm tall with 12 mm cells, and the platen moves 12 mm per
step.** Each step pushes exactly one grid height of fruit out of the bottom, and a
wire sweeping just under the grid cuts it off as a layer of 12 mm cubes. The tips
and the outside of each berry come out as smaller pieces. The last 12 mm stays in
the grid until the next batch pushes it out.

## How much the top takes

<Chart sim="waste" />

**A 5 mm cut takes about 15 % of a medium berry and 22 % of a small one (EST).**
The leafy end is the widest part, so every millimetre there is a lot of fruit. A
knife that cones out just the hull loses less. The fence moves in 1 mm steps from 3 to 9 mm. Start at 4 or 5 mm
and see what is left on the tops.

<details>
<summary>The detail</summary>

- **Why a wire.** Egg slicers cut strawberries cleanly, and a wire has no edge to
  keep sharp. Both cutting wires are 0.5 mm stainless (EST gauge) on stainless
  frames. Only the grid needs sharpening, because a wire grid would sag under the
  press.
- **Why the berries lie down.** Lying on its side, a berry is supported along its
  length and the fence finds its leafy end. Standing them up tip-down in holes
  would let tall berries stick up more than short ones, and the cut would no
  longer follow each berry.
- **Orientation in the hopper.** Once topped, a berry is just fruit: it can land on
  its side, its cut face or its tip, and the cubes come out the same.
- **The shape model.** The blunt leafy end is half a flattened ellipsoid,
  W/2 across and 0.3 W deep. The rest is a cone, with the length 1.2 W. The real
  shoulders vary, which is why the cut distance is adjustable.

</details>
