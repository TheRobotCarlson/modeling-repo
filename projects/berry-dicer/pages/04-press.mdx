**Tip the topped berries in, up to three rows (27 berries), then turn the handle
one click and push the wire rod across.** Pull the rod back on the next click. A
berry 30 mm tall takes three clicks. Cubes fall into a baking dish underneath.

<ModelViewer assembly="press" height={540} show={["Berries"]} />

## How hard it is to turn

**About 37 N on the handle at the worst moment, the force of lifting a 4 kg
bag (EST).** The peak comes when most berries are at their widest in the grid
at once. The handle is 215 mm long on a 15 mm pinion, about 14 to 1. If the
cutting force is twice the estimate, it is 75 N: press two rows instead of three.
See [time and force](../physics/).

<details>
<summary>The detail</summary>

- **Clicks.** The pinion has 16 teeth on a 15.3 mm pitch radius, so one turn moves
  the rack 96 mm: eight clicks of 12 mm on the index wheel and its spring plunger.
  The teeth are not drawn in the model.
- **The wire bow** is a flat stainless U. Its arms run in slots in the side walls,
  and the wire stretches across their tips 0.6 mm under the grid. A 6 mm
  stainless rod with a knob comes out at the front. The crossbar passes over the dish.
- **The cartridge** (hopper, grid, platen, bow) lifts out. The hopper's top flange
  sits on the side walls, and the platen bolts to the stem's foot with two
  stainless screws. Everything above the hopper is dry: no grease on the stem.
- **Dish.** Anything under 75 mm tall and at least 320 × 170 mm. A 9 × 13 in
  baking dish fits between the walls.

</details>
