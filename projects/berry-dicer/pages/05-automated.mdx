**The same stainless parts, with the topper tray mounted behind the hopper and
motors in place of the handle and the wire rod.** A click of the button (or
**Top a row** in Home Assistant) tops the row and tips it into the hopper. After up
to three rows, a long press (or **Dice**) steps the platen down and sweeps the wire
after each step. Your part is laying the berries in the tray.

<ModelViewer assembly="auto" height={560} show={["Berries"]} />

## One cycle

1. **Top.** A servo swings the topper wire down through the slot, and it stays
   there.
2. **Drop the tops.** A second servo flips the fence flap back. The tops fall into
   the compost bin, and the flap shuts again.
3. **Tip.** A third servo tilts the tray forward about its front edge to 135°.
   The row slides into the hopper. The tray tips under the parked platen, and the
   model is tested at 45°, 90° and 135°.
4. **Dice.** Two lead screws bring the platen down fast to 48 mm above the grid.
   Then it moves in 12 mm steps, and the belt sweeps the wire bow across after
   each one, alternating direction. Then the platen goes back up.

<details>
<summary>Motors, force and firmware</summary>

- **The platen** runs on two NEMA 17s on T8 lead screws with a **2 mm** lead,
  wired to two TMC2209s that share one step and one direction pin. Together they
  push about 700 N, against a peak of 420 N (EST). On the common 8 mm lead they
  would manage about 350 N and stall. One 12 mm step takes about a second.
- **The wire bow** is clamped to a GT2 belt along the left wall, on a third
  stepper.
- **Servos:** three in the 25 kg·cm class, for the topper arm, the tray and the
  flap. Their angles are placeholders in the firmware, to set on the bench.
- **Firmware:** `firmware/dicer.yaml`, ESPHome on a XIAO ESP32S3. On boot it homes
  the platen to its top switch and the bow to its front switch. It shows up in
  Home Assistant with **Top a row**, **Dice**, **Home** and a status line. It
  compiles with ESPHome 2026.10 and has not run.
- **Pins:** D0 / D1 platen step and direction, D2 / D3 bow, D4 – D6 servos, D7 the
  button, D8 / D9 home switches, D10 the lid. That is every pin on the XIAO.

</details>

<Callout title="It needs a lid before it runs.">
  Moving wires and a platen that pushes 700 N are not for fingers. A clear cover over the
  hopper and tray, with a switch that cuts the 24 V to the drivers and servos when it
  opens, is part of the design. The firmware also reads the switch and stops between
  moves. The cover is not modeled yet.
</Callout>
