---
title: Cleaning and food contact
summary: Stainless for everything the fruit touches; PLA Pure for the frames.
---

**The fruit only touches 304 stainless: the hopper, grid, platen, bow and wire.**
All of it lifts out and goes in the dishwasher. The frame, stem, handle and index
wheel are PLA Pure. They sit outside the
fruit's path and get wiped down.

<details>
<summary>Why not printed parts near the food</summary>

- **Heat.** PLA Pure's heat deflection temperature is listed at 56 °C (ISO 75,
  1.8 MPa), with a Vicat softening point of 63 °C. A dishwasher runs hotter, so the
  printed parts would warp.
- **Food contact.** Bambu says PLA Pure is food-contact tested at ingredient level
  and follows EU 10/2011 guidance. That covers the plastic, not a print. Layer
  lines and infill hold juice and are hard to clean. Here no printed part touches
  fruit, so it does not matter.
- **Stainless.** 304 is the usual food-service grade. SendCutSend laser cuts it
  from 0.030 to 0.5 in and bends it. Sharpen the grid strips on your whetstones
  before assembly. Laser-cut edges are square and leave a little dross, so
  deburr everything else with a file.

</details>

## Washing up

1. **Lift out the cartridge** (hopper with the grid in it), the platen and the bow.
   The platen comes off two screws.
2. **Push the last layer out of the grid** with the next batch, or with a spatula at
   the end of the session.
3. **Dishwasher**, or a brush under the tap. The grid's corners are the place
   juice collects. The cartridge is about 320 × 170 × 75 mm and fits a bottom
   rack.
4. **Check the wire** for tension and nicks now and then. A spare length of wire is
   in the parts list.
