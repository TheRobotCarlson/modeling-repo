---
title: What to measure
summary: Two numbers settle most of the design; a kitchen scale and a ruler get them.
---

**Measure the cutting force before ordering steel.** It takes one strawberry, a
kitchen scale and a few minutes, and it sets how hard the handle is.

| Test | How | Model says |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting force per mm of edge | Stand a berry on a piece of grid (or a 0.76 mm sheet edge) on a kitchen scale; push it through and note the peak; divide by the edge length in the cut | 0.1 N/mm (EST) |
| Wire cuts cleanly | An egg slicer, or a length of the wire, through a few berries | Clean; no tearing |
| Hulling by hand | Pinch the leaves off ten berries; time it and weigh what comes off | About 2 s and 2 mm each |
| Berry sizes | Width and length of a week's berries | 24 to 36 mm wide, length 1.1 to 1.3 times width |
| Hand force on the press | Luggage scale on the knob | 37 N at peak |
| Time | Stopwatch: knife week vs press week | 11 vs 5 min for 60 berries |
| Other foods | The scale test with whatever else you want to dice | Raw roots several times harder (EST) |

## First things to make

1. **A grid coupon.** Five strips, 60 mm long, slotted and sharpened. Push berries
   through on the scale. This checks the cutting force, the sharpening and the
   cube size in one go.
2. **The press.** Print the frame and stem first, then order the stainless.
