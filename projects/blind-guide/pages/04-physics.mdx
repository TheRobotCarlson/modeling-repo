---
title: Will it slide
summary: How steep the ramp has to be for the bar to slip off it instead of sitting on it.
---

**A 60° ramp sheds the bar unless the surface is very grippy: it works up to a
friction coefficient of about 1.6.** Hard plastic against a metal or plastic bar is
usually well under 0.6, so there is a wide margin. Rubbery TPU can be near or above
1, which is why the ramp is not made of it.

Where the blue line is below the orange one, the bar slides.

<details>
<summary>The detail</summary>

Two things hold the bar on the ramp. Friction is one. The other is the shade
itself: it hangs like a pendulum, and pushing the bar sideways makes it pull back
towards where it wants to hang. For a bar pushed *x* sideways on a drop of length
*L* below the roller, that pull is the bar's weight times *x / L*.

Balancing forces along and across a ramp tilted θ from horizontal, the bar slides when

θ > atan(μ) + atan(x / L)

The first term is the friction angle. The second is the pendulum's, and it is
small: 1.7° for a 4.5 mm push on a 150 mm drop. The bar's weight cancels out.

Not modelled: the fabric's own stiffness, the bar tilting as one end is pushed, and
the motor still paying out fabric while the bar sits on the ramp. The friction
values quoted above are typical ranges, not measurements of these parts.

</details>
