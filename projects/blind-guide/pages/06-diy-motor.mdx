---
title: Build your own motor
summary: Printed bead-chain motors that could replace the AXIS Gears, from MakerWorld and GitHub.
---

**Several printed motors pull a roller shade's bead chain the same way the AXIS Gear does, and four are on MakerWorld.**
The AXIS Gear is a motor in a box that grips the shade's looped chain and pulls it, just as a
hand would. It has also been discontinued. A DIY motor does the same job with an ESP32 running
ESPHome, so it joins Home Assistant like the [remotes](../../ha-remote/), with no cloud account.

<Callout title="Research only.">
  None of these has been printed or tried on your shades. The chain's bead size and how hard
  the shade is to pull are not measured yet, and both decide which design works.
</Callout>

## The candidates

**Start with the NEMA 17 designs; the 28BYJ-48 ones are cheaper but often too weak for a full-size shade.**

| Design | Motor | Driver and board | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Minimalistic Motorized Roller Blinds](https://makerworld.com/en/models/213425) (MakerWorld; also [Printables](https://www.printables.com/model/465889)) | NEMA 17 stepper, 0.42 Nm | TMC2209 | The driver senses a stall, so the motor finds the shade's end stops without switches. The author warns its pull may not be enough for every shade. A Fusion 360 file lets you change the sprocket for your chain. |
| [bead-chain-blind-motor](https://github.com/jamesrklau/bead-chain-blind-motor) (GitHub) | NEMA 17 | ESP32, TMC2209, ESPHome | Nothing on the shade changes: a printed sprocket grips the existing chain. Made for 6 mm beads. Most thoroughly documented. |
| [Smart Roller Blind Motor](https://makerworld.com/en/models/3292116) (MakerWorld) | 12 V worm-gear motor, 40 rpm | ESP32-C3, TB6612FNG | Under $20 a shade. It knows where the shade is only by timing the motor, with no encoder or end switches, so it can drift. A worm gear holds the shade still when the power is off. |
| [SmartBlinds Pro](https://makerworld.com/models/2217787) (MakerWorld) | 28BYJ-48 stepper | ESP32-C3 SuperMini, custom board | Powered by USB-C, works with bead chains. The cheapest motor, and the weakest. |
| [Geared brushless motor with encoder](https://makerworld.com/models/2995199) (MakerWorld) | Brushless gear motor with encoder | ESP32, ESPHome | The encoder counts turns, so it knows where the shade is. |

<details>
<summary>Why the motor size matters</summary>

Forum builders report the 28BYJ-48 barely moves a small shade and skips steps on a 52-inch
one, even with extra gearing. Most then switched to a NEMA 17. A NEMA 17 has several times
the torque, but it needs 12 V and a proper driver, so each shade needs a wall adapter rather
than USB. The AXIS Gear was rated for shades up to 9 × 9 ft and 10 lb, which is the bar to
clear.

The [Automatic Smart Roller Blinds Mod](https://makerworld.com/en/models/879069) (NEMA 17,
ESP32-C3) is left out of the table because it means taking the shade's clutch apart. The
others leave the shade as it is.

</details>

## How this ties to the guide

**A DIY motor could make the [guide cap](../guide/) unnecessary.** The bottom bar catches on
the AXIS Gear's top. A printed housing can be shaped so that the bar slides off it, or be
narrower so it clears the gap entirely. That is cheaper to get right before printing than
afterwards.

<details>
<summary>What to measure first</summary>

- **The bead chain:** bead diameter, the gap from one bead to the next, and the loop length.
  The sprocket is drawn from these.
- **The pull:** hang a luggage scale on the chain and read how hard it pulls to lift the
  shade from fully down. This picks the motor.
- **Power:** whether there's an outlet near each window, or the motor must run on a battery
  and solar panel like the AXIS Gear.

</details>
