**Per shade: a 12 V adapter feeds the stepper driver directly and the ESP32 through a small converter, and three wires from the ESP32 drive the motor.**
This follows the [original design](https://github.com/jamesrklau/bead-chain-blind-motor) with two
changes: the driver's enable pin goes to the ESP32, so the motor is only powered while moving,
and one adapter powers everything.

## Prints

**Three files from the original repo, in PETG.** 0.2 mm layers, at least 3 walls, about 40% infill.
The sprocket needs no supports.

<details>
<summary>The files, and printing the sprocket first</summary>

- `cad/sprocket/bead_chain_sprocket_test_plate.stl`: three 12-tooth sprockets with bead pockets
  of 4.4, 4.8 and 5.2 mm. **Print this first** and hang each on your chain: the one that holds
  the beads without them riding up is the one to use. A 5 mm D-shaped bore, held by an M3 set screw.
- `cad/case/case_body.stl`: motor channel, board holders, vents, a chain guard and wall ears.
- `cad/case/case_lid.stl`.

The author marks the case "not yet test-printed". Expect to adjust it, and it may be worth
redrawing here in build123d once the sprocket fits, so it clears the shade's bottom bar.

</details>

## Wiring

**Five connections to the ESP32, and 12 V to the driver board.**

| From | To | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ESP32 GPIO18 | Driver STEP | One pulse per step |
| ESP32 GPIO19 | Driver DIR | Direction |
| ESP32 GPIO21 | Driver EN | Low = motor powered. Off when idle, so it runs cool |
| ESP32 3V3 | Driver logic supply (VIO) | 3.3 V logic. Not 5 V |
| ESP32 GND | Driver GND | Shared ground |
| 12 V adapter | Expansion board's screw terminal | Motor power |
| 12 V → 5 V buck converter | ESP32 5V (VIN) and GND | One adapter for everything |

<details>
<summary>Setting the driver up</summary>

- **Microstepping:** leave the driver's MS1/MS2 jumpers at their default (eighth steps): 1,600
  steps per motor turn. One turn moves 72 mm of chain (12 beads × 6 mm), so about 22 steps per mm.
- **Current:** set with the trimmer on the driver. Start at about 0.8 A, half the motor's rating:
  enough for most shades, and it stays cool. Raise it only if the motor skips. The driver's own
  manual gives the trimmer voltage for each current.
- **Motor wires:** if the shade moves the wrong way, don't rewire. Set `dir_inverted: "true"` in
  the shade's YAML.
- **Holding:** with the motor off when idle, the shade's own clutch holds it, as it does when you
  let go of the chain. If a shade creeps down, set the EN pin to always on (remove `sleep_pin`).

</details>

## Mounting

**Screw the case to the wall below the shade's chain end, so the loop wraps the sprocket and stays slightly taut.**
Too slack and the beads jump teeth; too tight and the motor works harder and the shade's clutch
wears. Slotted screw holes, or a spacer, make the tension adjustable.
