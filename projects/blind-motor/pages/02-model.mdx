**Three prints: a box that screws to the wall, a lid the motor bolts to, and a sprocket on the motor's shaft.**
Drag to turn the model; the slider takes it apart. The chain is on its own layer.

## Inside, bottom to top

**Motor at the bottom so the sprocket sits low, cells at the top, boards beside the motor.**

- **Gearbox and motor:** the gearbox's face bolts to the lid with two M3 screws, so the shaft is
  always square to the lid. The motor can and its encoder stand up from the gearbox.
- **Boards:** stand on edge in the gaps either side of the can. On the left are the XIAO and the
  buck converter, on the right the motor driver.
- **Cells:** two 18650s side by side on a printed floor, with a spring contact under and over each.
- **Charger:** lies flat on top of the cells, its USB-C through the right-hand wall.
- **Buttons:** two, up and down, poke through the top.

<details>
<summary>The sprocket</summary>

12 pockets for #10 beads (4.5 mm on a 6 mm pitch) put the bead centres on an 11.59 mm radius,
so one turn moves 72 mm of chain. A groove between the pockets takes the wire that joins the
beads, and a flange each side keeps the chain on. The bore is D-shaped to the motor's shaft, with
an M3 set screw down onto the flat.

Print it first, on its own. If the beads ride up out of the pockets, the pocket or pitch is
wrong for your chain: measure C1 and C2.

</details>

## Every size, by code

<Drawing name="box" />

<details>
<summary>Which sizes matter most</summary>

- **B4, where the chain runs.** The shade's chain hangs where it hangs; the box has to put
  its sprocket on that line. A spacer behind the box can push it further out.
- **M8 and M9, the shaft's height and the mounting holes.** No listing gives them. They set where
  the lid's holes go, so measure them on the motor before printing the lid.
- **M5, the motor can plus encoder.** Encoder caps vary. If it's longer than 42.8 mm, the box
  grows by the difference.

</details>
