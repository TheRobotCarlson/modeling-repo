**Two remotes on the XIAO ESP32S3 + Wio-SX1262 kit: a puck with one big button,
and a bar with five.** Each sleeps at about 20 µA, wakes on a press, reports the
button to Home Assistant through ESPHome, and goes back to sleep. Both charge over
USB-C.

## The idea in one print

**Each remote's top, buttons and springs come off the printer as one part.** The
shell is PLA. The buttons are rigid PLA caps held in a 75A TPU panel. A U-shaped
channel in the TPU around each cap is its spring and its seal. Dissolvable support
fills the gaps during the print, so nothing needs assembling but the base.

## Where this stands

1. **[The puck](./puck/)**: one 31 mm button over a 12 mm switch. Modeled.
2. **[The bar](./bar/)**: a main button and four keys. Modeled.
3. **[Wiring](./wiring/)**: two wires per button, a battery, and two resistors to read its voltage.
4. **[Firmware](./firmware/)**: ESPHome configs for both, over Wi-Fi or LoRa. Config-checked.
5. **[Battery life](./power/)**: about 190 days on Wi-Fi, 340 on LoRa, at ten presses a day.

<details>
<summary>Why not just use the radio kit's own button?</summary>

The Wio-SX1262 board has a user button on GPIO21, and that is the cheapest
single-button remote there is. Its position on the board is not published, so a
cap cannot be designed over it yet. It is also small and sits between the boards.
A separate 12 mm switch gives a bigger, firmer press and lets the kit sit wherever
the USB port needs it.

</details>
