**A slim bar with one round main button and a 2 × 2 block of keys.** The same
construction as the puck: a single TPU panel carries all five caps, each in its own
spring channel, and five 6 mm tact switches sit on pedestals underneath.

<ModelViewer assembly="bar" height={560} />

## Layout

**Buttons at the top, battery in the middle, the kit and USB-C at the bottom.**
That keeps the heavy part in the hand and the charging cable out of the way. The
antennas lie along the side walls beside the battery, where nothing else fits.
**Show wiring**, under the model, adds the wires: a six-wire ribbon up the middle
to the keys, and the battery leads straight into the kit. See [wiring](../wiring/).

| Cap | Size | Pin | Event `button` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main (orange) | 17 mm round | D1 / GPIO2 | 1 |
| Key 1, top left | 11 mm square | D2 / GPIO3 | 2 |
| Key 2, top right | 11 mm square | D3 / GPIO4 | 3 |
| Key 3, bottom left | 11 mm square | D4 / GPIO5 | 4 |
| Key 4, bottom right | 11 mm square | D5 / GPIO6 | 5 |

Caps are separate colours in the same print. Swap the colours to suit what the
buttons do.

## Feedback

**The same buzz and blink as the puck: once when Home Assistant has the press,
three times when it did not.** The motor and its driver sit on top of the cell,
under the keys, and the light pipe rises over the XIAO's LED.

**The bar has no accelerometer, because it has no pins left.** The five buttons,
the radio and the battery take nine of the XIAO's eleven edge pins, and the motor a
tenth. An accelerometer needs two more for its bus, plus a wake line.

<details>
<summary>Ways to add one later</summary>

The bus needs two pins:

- **Give up one key.** Its pin and D7 become the bus.
- **Or move the motor to an I2C port expander.** D6 and D7 become the bus for the
  expander and the accelerometer. It costs about a microamp asleep, and more wiring.

In both cases the accelerometer's interrupt, set to open-drain, shares the main
button's line, and the firmware tells a press from a movement by asking the chip
after it wakes.

</details>

## Holding the base on

**Screws alone would let the far end of the base bend away when the top buttons are
pressed.** So the base has a tongue at the top end that hooks into a slot in the
shell's end wall. Hook the tongue in, swing the base shut, and fit four screws: two
at the bottom end and two beside the keys.

<details>
<summary>The detail</summary>

- Panel: 33 × 57.5 mm. Channels are narrower than the puck's (2.2 mm, 0.7 mm walls,
  2.2 mm deep) to fit between the keys. The web between neighbouring channels is
  0.6 mm at the surface.
- The two upper screw bosses stop 3 mm below the top so they clear the keypad's
  channels; ribs tie them to the side walls.
- Switch: 6 × 6 × 5 mm. The stems rest 0.2 mm above each switch.

</details>
