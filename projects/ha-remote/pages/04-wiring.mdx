**Each button is a switch between one XIAO pin and ground; the battery goes on the
XIAO's battery pads; three resistors on D0 read both the battery and USB power.**
The motor hangs off one more pin through a transistor, and the puck adds an
accelerometer on I2C. The radio kit is used as it comes.

| Pin | Puck | Bar |
|---|---|---|
| D0 (GPIO1) | Battery and USB sense | Battery and USB sense |
| D1 (GPIO2) | Button | Main button (1) |
| D2 – D5 (GPIO3 – 6) | Accelerometer INT, motor, SDA, SCL | Keys 1 – 4 (2 – 5) |
| D6 (GPIO43) | Free | Motor |
| D7 (GPIO44) | Free | Free |
| D8 – D10 | The radio's SPI bus | The radio's SPI bus |

| From | To | Note |
|---|---|---|
| LiPo + / − | XIAO `BAT+` / `BAT−` pads | Negative is the pad nearer the USB port (Seeed wiki) |
| Each button | Its pin and GND | One GND wire can daisy-chain all switches |
| 1 MΩ | `BAT+` to D0 | Top of the divider |
| 1 MΩ + 100 nF | D0 to GND | Bottom of the divider; the capacitor steadies the reading. Any ground will do: the models use the nearest switch's ground leg |
| 2.2 MΩ | `5V` pad to D0 | USB sense: on USB, D0 reads above 2.1 V; on the battery alone, never above 1.7 V |
| Motor + | `3V3` | With a 1N4148 across the motor, stripe to `3V3` |
| Motor − | PN2222A collector | Emitter to GND |
| 1 kΩ | Motor pin to the PN2222A base | 1 MΩ from base to emitter keeps the motor off while the pin floats in sleep |
| Accelerometer `3Vo`, GND, SDA, SCL, INT | `3V3`, GND, D4, D5, D2 | Puck only. Feed `3Vo`, not `Vin`, to skip the board's regulator |

**The models carry every wire, as one way to run them.** Red is battery +, black
is ground, orange is 3V3 and 5V, white is the sense line to D0, yellow the
button and motor lines, blue the accelerometer, purple the motor leads.
While the wiring shows, the printed parts turn see-through. Taking the model apart
hides the wiring, because the parts it joins move away from each other. On the
puck and bar pages the wiring starts hidden; **Show wiring** under the model turns
it on.

<details>
<summary>How the wires are run</summary>

- **Battery leads** go from the cell's end into the 2 mm gap between the two
  boards and up to the battery pads underneath the XIAO. In the bar that is a
  straight run; in the puck the leads climb over the cell and come round in front
  of the switch. The stops between cell and kit are split so the leads pass
  between them.
- **Button wires** leave the XIAO's edge pads, climb over the USB-C port and run
  as a flat ribbon at 10.9 mm, clear of the battery leads below. Wires that cross
  the ribbon do it at 12 mm. Each drops to
  its switch's side. In the bar, ground runs up the same ribbon and branches to
  every switch.
- **The divider** lies on the floor beside the battery (bar) or left of the
  switch (puck). Its bottom end goes to the nearest switch's ground leg.
- **The motor driver** is a transistor, two resistors and a diode soldered into
  one bundle and sleeved in heat-shrink. In the puck it sits on the floor right of
  the kit, with the motor beside the switch. In the bar both sit on top of the
  cell, under the keys.
- **The accelerometer's five wires** (puck) leave the kit's left pads, run down
  the left of the switch with the button wire and fan out over the board onto its
  header. 3V3 and ground cross over from the right-hand pads 1.1 mm higher.
- 30 AWG silicone wire is taken as 0.7 mm across. The models are tested so that
  no wire touches another part except at its ends. The routes are a plan; the battery pad
  position is estimated (see the callout).

</details>

<Callout title="Check before soldering: where the battery pads are with the radio board fitted.">
  On the plain XIAO the battery pads are on the underside, and in this kit the
  Wio-SX1262 board is stacked against that side. Find the pads, or a battery
  connection on the Wio board, before cutting wires.
</Callout>

## Why these pins

**Waking from deep sleep needs an RTC-capable pin, and on the ESP32-S3 those are
GPIO0 to GPIO21.** The radio takes D8 – D10 (GPIO7 – 9) for SPI, and its control
lines use GPIO38 – 42 through the board-to-board connector. That leaves D0 – D5
(GPIO1 – 6) as the wake-capable pins on the edge: one for the battery, five for
buttons in the bar, or a button and the accelerometer's interrupt in the puck. D6
and D7 (GPIO43 and 44) cannot wake the chip, which is fine for the motor.

<details>
<summary>The detail</summary>

- **Pull-ups.** The switches pull a pin to ground, so each pin needs a pull-up that
  stays on in deep sleep. ESPHome's multi-pin wake does not set one, so the firmware
  turns on the RTC pull-ups just before sleeping (`common/rtc_pullups.h`). If a
  remote wakes by itself, a 100 kΩ resistor from each button pin to 3V3 is the
  fallback.
- **GPIO3 is a strapping pin.** It only matters at power-on if JTAG has been
  configured in eFuses, which it has not on a stock XIAO. ESPHome warns about it
  anyway.
- **The divider** draws about 2.4 µA all the time. That is the price of knowing the
  charge level. The plain XIAO ESP32S3 has no battery-sense circuit of its own.
- **USB sense on the same pin.** With 2.2 MΩ from the 5V pad, D0 reads
  Vbat ÷ 2.45 on the battery (1.22 – 1.71 V) and (Vbat + 2.27) ÷ 2.45 on USB
  (2.15 – 2.64 V). The firmware calls anything above 1.93 V "on USB". Unplugged,
  the 5V pad sits near 0 V; if it does not, the threshold needs checking.
- **The motor pin floats in deep sleep.** The 1 MΩ from base to emitter holds the
  transistor off until the firmware drives the pin.
- **GPIO21 is the user LED and the Wio board's button.** Pressing that button lights
  the LED. Driving the LED is safe because the button is shut inside the case.
- **Kit pins**, from Meshtastic's `seeed_xiao_s3` variant: NSS 41, RESET 42, BUSY
  40, DIO1 39, SCK 7, MISO 8, MOSI 9, RF switch on DIO2 (also wired to GPIO38),
  TCXO at 1.8 V on DIO3.

</details>

## The radio must be put to sleep

**The SX1262 powers up in standby, drawing about 0.6 mA: forty times the ESP32's
own deep sleep.** Left alone, it flattens the battery in under three weeks, even in
the Wi-Fi version that never uses the radio. Both firmware configs send it to cold
sleep as the last thing before every deep sleep. See [battery life](../power/).
