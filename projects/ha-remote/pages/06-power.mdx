---
title: Battery life
summary: On Wi-Fi the presses set it; on LoRa the sleep current does.
---

**With the 350 mAh cell, about 180 days per charge on Wi-Fi at ten presses a day, and
300 on LoRa.** Sitting untouched, either one lasts a little under a year. The
numbers are for the puck; the bar has no accelerometer and sleeps 2 µA lower. All of the
currents are estimates or datasheet figures, not measurements.

## One press

<Chart sim="press" />

**A Wi-Fi press costs about seven times the charge of a LoRa press.** Most of it is
joining the network and waiting for Home Assistant to connect. On LoRa the
feedback buzz is now the biggest part: the packet itself is on air for 31 ms, the
motor runs for 120.

## Days per charge

<Chart sim="life" />

**On Wi-Fi, presses cost as much as sleeping from about ten a day upwards. On
LoRa, sleep dominates until about seventy a day.** Of the 36 µA asleep
(self-discharge included), the ESP32-S3's own deep sleep is 14 µA. The bottom line is what happens if the radio is
never put to sleep: 19 days.

<Params sim="life" />

**Waking on motion is the one feature that can cost more than the presses.** Each
pick-up without a press joins Wi-Fi and waits four seconds: about 0.11 mAh. Ten
presses and ten pick-ups a day bring the puck down to about 108 days.
[Firmware](../firmware/) says how to turn it off.

<details>
<summary>The model</summary>

Days per charge = usable charge ÷ (sleep current × 24 h + presses per day ×
charge per press). Usable charge is 85 % of 350 mAh. The sleep current adds the
XIAO's deep sleep (14 µA, Seeed), the SX1262 in sleep (0.6 µA, ESPHome docs), the
battery and USB sense resistors (2.4 µA), the accelerometer in low-power mode
(2 µA, MSA311 datasheet, typical), a typical cell protection chip (3 µA, estimate) and
self-discharge of 3 % a month, counted as a current (14 µA). A press is a sequence
of fixed-current phases; the LoRa time on air uses Semtech's formula for SF7,
125 kHz, a 6-byte payload. The buzz is the motor's 60 mA at 3 V (Adafruit) for
120 ms; the LED's few milliamps are left out. The script is `physics/power.py`.

A USB power meter between a charger and the remote measures the press side.
Sleep current needs a meter on the battery lead with a µA range.

</details>
