**An optional third device: the same radio kit, an SHT40 sensor and a cell,
waking on a timer to report temperature and humidity.** It is kept out of the remotes
on purpose. A sensor inside something held in the hand reads the hand, and
reporting on a timer would cost the remotes most of their battery. This page is a
spec: no CAD and no firmware yet.

## How long it lasts

<Chart sim="sensor" />

**On LoRa, reporting every ten minutes lasts about six months per charge. On Wi-Fi
it lasts under a month.** Each Wi-Fi report has to join the network and wait for
Home Assistant, and that costs twelve times a LoRa packet. On Wi-Fi, report
every 30 minutes or more, or power it from USB.

| Part | Why | Link |

| 2 × 1 MΩ, 100 nF | Battery divider on D0, as in the remotes | See [parts](../parts/) |

A LoRa build also needs the [gateway](../firmware/), which the remotes can share.

<details>
<summary>The spec</summary>

- **Power the sensor from a pin.** Feed the SHT40 board from a GPIO, switched on
  only to take a reading. The board's regulator and any power LED then draw nothing
  asleep, and the sleep current stays at about 34 µA (self-discharge included),
  the same as a remote without its extras.
- **Pins.** I2C on D4 and D5, sensor power on D3, battery on D0. No buttons and no
  wake pins: a timer wakes it (`deep_sleep` with `sleep_duration`).
- **Report.** Over LoRa, a new packet type: marker 0xA6, node id, temperature ×100
  (signed 16 bits), humidity ×100, battery mV and a sequence number, 9 bytes. The
  gateway's packet length is fixed at 6 bytes today, so the gateway config has to
  change to accept both. Over Wi-Fi, plain ESPHome sensors.
- **Enclosure.** The sensor sits behind vents, away from the board and the cell,
  which warm up while awake. A printed louvred case with the SHT40 at the top, or on
  a short lead outside, both work. Not designed yet.
- **The model behind the chart.** The remotes' sleep current without the USB sense
  resistor and the accelerometer, plus one report per interval. A report is a
  remote's press without the buzz, plus about 20 ms to power and read the sensor.
  `physics/power.py`, chart 3.

</details>
