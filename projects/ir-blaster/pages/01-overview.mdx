**One box per room, up high, sends IR in every direction, so Home Assistant can press any remote's buttons.**
Inside are an ESP32 running ESPHome, a ring of infrared LEDs and a temperature sensor. The
codes come from remotes already saved on your Flipper Zero. The TV and lights show up in Home
Assistant as buttons, and the mini-split as presets or a full thermostat.

## What already exists

**IR blasters are common and cheap, so the case for building one rests on three things a store-bought one lacks.**

- **Store-bought:** a Broadlink RM4 mini or a SwitchBot Hub Mini costs about $25–35 and works with
  Home Assistant. But the AC appears only as buttons, not as a thermostat, and Broadlink's
  setup wants its app and cloud account first.
- **Printed DIY:** MakerWorld has a [customizable IR blaster case for the ESP32-C3 SuperMini](https://makerworld.com/en/models/1606154-customizable-ir-blaster-case-esp32-c3-supermini)
  with an ESPHome config, and an [ESP-01 IR blaster](https://www.makerworld.com/en/models/2000408)
  made for ACs whose Wi-Fi module died. GitHub has an
  [IR sniffer and blaster](https://github.com/jimbottle/ir-sniffer-blaster) that records a remote and replays it.
  Each has a single LED, good for one device it faces, not a whole room.

What this build adds:

1. **A real thermostat.** ESPHome's [IR climate](https://esphome.io/components/climate/climate_ir/) component
   knows the full AC protocol for many mini-split brands, so Home Assistant gets a thermostat entity.
   It can also take the room's temperature from the box's own sensor rather than the indoor unit's,
   which reads hot air near the ceiling.
2. **Whole-room coverage.** Several LEDs pointing different ways, so it reaches the AC, the TV and the
   lights from one spot.
3. **All local.** ESPHome, like the [remotes](../ha-remote/): no app, no cloud account.

## The codes come from the Flipper

**Every device already answers your Flipper Zero, so it is IR-controllable, and the Flipper's saved
remotes become the box's buttons.** The Flipper stores each remote as a `.ir` text file. A short script
can turn those files into ESPHome config, so no codes need learning a second time.

<details>
<summary>How a Flipper recording becomes an ESPHome button</summary>

A Flipper `.ir` file holds one block per button, in one of two forms:

- **Parsed:** the Flipper recognised the protocol and saved just the address and command, e.g.
  `protocol: NEC`, `address: 04 00 00 00`, `command: 08 00 00 00`. This becomes ESPHome's
  `remote_transmitter.transmit_nec` (or `_samsung`, `_rc5` …) with the same numbers. Most TVs and
  cheap light remotes land here.
- **Raw:** the Flipper saved the carrier frequency and the pulse timings in microseconds,
  alternating on and off. That maps one-to-one onto `transmit_raw` with `carrier_frequency`: the
  same list, with every second number made negative. AC remotes usually land here, as their
  messages are long.

Each block becomes a `button:` in Home Assistant named as on the Flipper ("TV Power", "Lights Dim").

</details>

<details>
<summary>The mini-split: presets from the Flipper, or a full thermostat</summary>

An AC remote doesn't send "temperature up". Every press sends the *whole* state: mode, setpoint,
fan, swing. So a Flipper recording of one press always means exactly that state, e.g. "cool, 23°,
fan auto". Two ways to use it:

- **Presets, from the Flipper:** record the handful of states you actually use (off, cool 23, heat
  20, dry) and each becomes a button or a scene. Simple, and works for any brand.
- **Full thermostat:** if the brand has an ESPHome `climate_ir` platform (Mitsubishi, Daikin,
  Fujitsu, LG, Toshiba, Gree, Midea/Coolix, Panasonic and more; `heatpumpir` adds dozens), Home
  Assistant gets a real thermostat with any setpoint, driven by the box's own temperature sensor. The
  Flipper recording helps here too: its timings identify the protocol.

Start with presets; switch to the thermostat once the brand is known.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does the box still need a receiver?</summary>

**Not to learn codes: the Flipper does that.** A receiver (a TSOP38238, under a dollar) is now only
for one thing: catching presses from the old remotes, so Home Assistant knows someone turned the AC
up by hand. IR is one-way, so without it Home Assistant assumes its last command still holds. It's
cheap enough to keep a footprint for it and decide later.

</details>

## The box

**A puck on the ceiling or the top of a shelf, LEDs round its rim and one pointing down.**
IR bounces off walls and ceilings, so one bright box up high usually reaches everything in a room.

<details>
<summary>The electronics, first pass</summary>

- **Board:** a Seeed XIAO ESP32-C3, small and on USB-C. (The remotes use the XIAO ESP32S3; the C3 is
  enough here and cheaper.)
- **LEDs:** five or six 940 nm IR LEDs. Vishay's TSAL6200 is bright with a narrow ±17° beam for
  reach. The TSAL6400 (±25°) covers more angle per LED.
- **Drive:** an ESP32 pin can't power LEDs this bright, so it switches a logic-level MOSFET (AO3400A).
  At 5 V, a 33 Ω resistor per LED gives about 100 mA in each pulse. That figure is still to check
  against the datasheet's pulse rating.
- **Receiver (optional):** a TSOP38238, 38 kHz, the carrier almost every remote uses. Only for
  noticing the old remotes; the Flipper does the learning. Its window must face out and stay shaded
  from the box's own LEDs.
- **Sensor:** an SHT40 in its own vented chamber, away from the board's heat, as in the
  [gateway](../ha-remote/gateway/).
- **Power:** a USB-C wall adapter. Pulses are short, so the average draw is small.

</details>

## Questions before modeling

**The Flipper files, plus three answers, set the design.**

<details open>
<summary>What I need from you</summary>

1. **The Flipper `.ir` files** for each remote (on the SD card under `infrared/`). Drop them in
   `projects/ir-blaster/ir/` and I'll write the script that turns them into ESPHome config.
2. **Mini-split brand and remote model,** for the full-thermostat option. The model number is usually
   on the back of the remote or inside its battery cover. Is it the same remote as the
   [AC remote cover](../ac-remote-cover/)?
3. **Rooms and mounting:** how many rooms, and ceiling, shelf top or wall?
4. **Power:** is there an outlet near where each box would go, or should it run from a USB port
   or a power strip?
